Notes from a call with a recent-graduate trader on a cross-commodities desk
at a CCI-style merchant energy trading firm:

- Hiring is broad: apply widely, interviews lean on market-focused case
  studies plus statistics and dataframe manipulation questions, with some
  quant-style questions mixed in.
- Interviewers also probe computer architecture / systems understanding,
  not just language syntax — they want to know you understand how your
  code actually runs, not just that it runs.
- Overall emphasis is more on math and market reasoning than on rote
  programming ability.
- Energy commodities, especially power, are described as a major current
  opportunity set for someone with quantitative skills.
- Before choosing a statistical method, you're expected to reason about the
  data-generating process and data frequency — e.g. why an hourly power
  price series behaves differently from a daily equity series.
- Traders increasingly expect junior quant hires to be able to explain why
  a method works, not just apply it: e.g. why ridge regression helps with
  collinearity, what regularization is doing to the optimization problem,
  whether a model is statistically sound.
- Expect some theoretical ML questions even in a desk-facing interview.
- Experienced traders are increasingly leaning on quantitative tools as
  markets get more complex; physical/causal intuition about the grid still
  matters, but simple one-step causal rules (e.g. "cold weather -> higher
  price") are less sufficient than they used to be — the interactions are
  more complex now.
- Anecdote: the interviewer's own boss once asked a theoretical modeling
  question in an interview that mapped directly onto a live research
  bottleneck the desk was dealing with; the candidate's theoretical answer
  was later actually used to make progress on the real problem. The
  candidate didn't know at the time it was a live issue — it just happened
  to be the right kind of thinking.
