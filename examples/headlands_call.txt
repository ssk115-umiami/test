Notes from call with a researcher at a Headlands-style systematic electronic
trading firm:

- The firm is research-led and trades almost entirely delta-one products:
  futures, equities, bonds. Fully systematic — software makes every trade,
  no human overrides on live strategies.
- Market data (order book / trade data) feeds directly into time-series and
  neural-network style research.
- Even though a lot of code generation is now AI-assisted internally,
  interviews are still very coding-heavy: expect LeetCode-style algorithm
  questions and standard quant math (probability, stats).
- Data-science-style interview round: you're handed a raw dataset and asked
  how you'd clean it, split it (they will push back hard on random splits
  for time series), model it, validate it, and reason about failure modes.
- Linear algebra questions go deep — proof-style reasoning at the level of
  a serious undergraduate course (Axler-style: rank, eigenvalues, SVD,
  positive-definiteness), not just "compute this matrix product."
- Day to day, researchers spend real time checking PnL, diagnosing why a
  production strategy underperformed, running historical simulation
  experiments, and modifying strategy/data/research pipeline code.
- Good research ideas often come from deeply understanding an existing
  codebase and production system, not from a blank-slate idea.
- Recommended project direction if you want to be taken seriously: build
  something using real historical market data plus a market-making or
  short-horizon prediction model — not another generic Kaggle-style ML demo.
- Projects get probed conversationally in interviews (they'll ask you to
  defend design choices) rather than read line-by-line, so be ready to
  explain every choice, not just recite results.
- This person's opinion, not confirmed firm policy: they believe
  research-generation work will increasingly be automated by LLM agents
  over the next few years, so judgment/debugging may matter more than raw
  coding speed over time. Treat this as one person's forecast, not a hiring
  requirement.
